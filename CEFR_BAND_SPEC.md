# CEFR Tester Trainer — Internal Band Specification

> The Council of Europe defines A1, A2, B1, B2, C1 and C2. The .1/.2 labels below are internal training bands for this simulator, not official CEFR levels. They are anchored to Range, Accuracy, Fluency, Interaction and Coherence.

## Band map

|---|---|---|---|
| **A1.1** | A1 | lower / emerging A1 | Cannot sustain a simple past/future account or explain a reason beyond a memorised because + short phrase. |
| **A1.2** | A1 | upper / consolidating A1 | Narratives are fragmentary and reasons stay concrete; unfamiliar comparison/hypothesis usually requires major simplification. |
| **A2.1** | A2 | lower / emerging A2 | Opinions are brief and minimally justified; hypotheticals and multi-step comparison quickly expose limits. |
| **A2.2** | A2 | upper / consolidating A2 | Limited subordinate-clause control and little nuance; difficult ideas are simplified rather than developed. |
| **B1.1** | B1 | lower / emerging B1 | Arguments lack detail/qualification; sustained hypotheticals and precise comparisons are inconsistent. |
| **B1.2** | B1 | upper / consolidating B1 | Precision, collocation, nuance and sustained abstract argument are not yet reliably B2. |
| **B2.1** | B2 | lower / emerging B2 | May become repetitive or imprecise when required to nuance, qualify or defend an abstract position at length. |
| **B2.2** | B2 | upper / consolidating B2 | Does not yet show consistently precise, nuanced and almost effortless control across unfamiliar domains expected at C1. |

## A1.1 — Very early A1: relies on isolated words, memorised chunks and very short personal answers.

**Official anchor:** A1 (lower/emerging half)

### Five spoken-performance dimensions

- **Range:** Very basic repertoire of words and simple phrases tied to concrete personal situations.
- **Accuracy:** Limited control of a few memorised structures; frequent basic mistakes.
- **Fluency:** Very short, isolated utterances with much pausing and repair.
- **Interaction:** Simple personal questions/answers only; heavily dependent on repetition and rephrasing.
- **Coherence:** Words or groups of words linked mainly with and/then.

### Typical interview behaviour

- **Comprehension:** Understands slow, concrete, familiar questions when wording is predictable; often needs repetition or simpler reformulation.
- **Grammar:** Mostly words and formulaic present-simple chunks. be/have/can, articles and basic word order are unstable.
- **Expected answer shape:** simple question — 1–5 words; familiar open question — one short clause, sometimes two; stretch question — fragment, misunderstanding or very short partial answer.
- **Error behaviour:** Basic errors are frequent and systematic; context often carries part of the meaning.
- **Upper boundary:** Cannot sustain a simple past/future account or explain a reason beyond a memorised because + short phrase.

### Useful tester probes

- **basic description:** “Describe your school/classroom or family.”  
  Expected: labels a few familiar things.  
  Ceiling signal: cannot elaborate without heavy support.
- **reason:** “Why do you like it?”  
  Expected: because + one simple idea.  
  Ceiling signal: reason remains memorised/repetitive.
- **time reference:** “What did you do yesterday?”  
  Expected: isolated event words/chunks.  
  Ceiling signal: past narration collapses.

### Adjacent-band discrimination

- **Why not lower?** No lower simulator band.
- **Why not higher?** A1.2 can usually produce several short sentences and cope with a small follow-up without immediate breakdown.

## A1.2 — Secure A1: can produce short simple sentences about immediate personal topics and survive brief predictable exchanges.

**Official anchor:** A1 (upper/consolidating half)

### Five spoken-performance dimensions

- **Range:** Basic repertoire of phrases for everyday personal needs with a little recombination.
- **Accuracy:** Some memorised structures are stable, but basic errors remain frequent.
- **Fluency:** Short connected bursts with obvious planning pauses.
- **Interaction:** Can manage a brief predictable exchange and a simple follow-up with support.
- **Coherence:** Can connect a few simple ideas with and/but/because.

### Typical interview behaviour

- **Comprehension:** Handles clear familiar questions but struggles when wording changes substantially or the idea becomes hypothetical.
- **Grammar:** Simple present, can, there is/are and some memorised past/future chunks; accuracy remains uneven.
- **Expected answer shape:** simple question — one simple sentence; familiar open question — 2–4 short sentences; stretch question — one partial idea plus hesitation/clarification.
- **Error behaviour:** Frequent tense, article, preposition and third-person -s errors, generally without blocking basic meaning.
- **Upper boundary:** Narratives are fragmentary and reasons stay concrete; unfamiliar comparison/hypothesis usually requires major simplification.

### Useful tester probes

- **routine vs event:** “What do you usually do after school? What did you do yesterday?”  
  Expected: distinguishes routine more reliably than past event.  
  Ceiling signal: past forms are sparse or unstable.
- **simple comparison:** “Which is better, school at home or at school? Why?”  
  Expected: one concrete preference and reason.  
  Ceiling signal: cannot develop or qualify comparison.
- **reformulation:** “Ask the same idea with less familiar wording.”  
  Expected: may recognise topic but need rephrasing.  
  Ceiling signal: meaning depends strongly on familiar question forms.

### Adjacent-band discrimination

- **Why not lower?** A1.1 is more dependent on isolated words/chunks and repetition.
- **Why not higher?** A2.1 can sustain several linked sentences and manage straightforward past/future reference more productively.

## A2.1 — Developing A2: manages short everyday exchanges and gives simple linked descriptions of familiar routines and events.

**Official anchor:** A2 (lower/emerging half)

### Five spoken-performance dimensions

- **Range:** Basic sentence patterns and memorised phrases for a growing range of everyday situations.
- **Accuracy:** Some simple structures correct, but basic mistakes remain systematic.
- **Fluency:** Short utterances are understandable despite obvious pauses, false starts and reformulation.
- **Interaction:** Can answer questions and respond to simple statements; rarely drives the conversation.
- **Coherence:** Links groups of words/sentences with simple connectors such as and/but/because/then.

### Typical interview behaviour

- **Comprehension:** Understands straightforward questions at clear conversational speed; abstract wording needs help.
- **Grammar:** Present forms are usable; past simple and basic future are emerging; subordinate clauses are limited.
- **Expected answer shape:** simple question — 1–2 sentences; familiar open question — 3–5 short linked sentences; stretch question — short simplified answer with visible searching.
- **Error behaviour:** Regular tense choice, irregular past, article and preposition errors; simple meaning remains clear.
- **Upper boundary:** Opinions are brief and minimally justified; hypotheticals and multi-step comparison quickly expose limits.

### Useful tester probes

- **short narrative:** “Tell me what happened last weekend from beginning to end.”  
  Expected: simple sequence with then/after.  
  Ceiling signal: tense control and detail are fragile.
- **future:** “What are you going to do next weekend?”  
  Expected: basic planned future.  
  Ceiling signal: limited variation beyond one familiar form.
- **opinion:** “Should children have homework every day?”  
  Expected: clear view + one or two simple reasons.  
  Ceiling signal: little development/counterargument.

### Adjacent-band discrimination

- **Why not lower?** A1.2 usually relies more on rehearsed chunks and shorter answers.
- **Why not higher?** A2.2 can maintain a short coherent account and paraphrase a missing everyday word more effectively.

## A2.2 — Strong A2: sustains simple conversation on familiar matters and can give a short coherent account with reasons and sequencing.

**Official anchor:** A2 (upper/consolidating half)

### Five spoken-performance dimensions

- **Range:** A usable basic repertoire across common everyday topics with limited paraphrase.
- **Accuracy:** Simple structures often work, but basic mistakes still recur under pressure.
- **Fluency:** Short stretches are reasonably continuous; hesitation rises sharply with complexity.
- **Interaction:** Can sustain a straightforward exchange and signal/repair misunderstanding.
- **Coherence:** Connects short sequences with a small but functional set of linking words.

### Typical interview behaviour

- **Comprehension:** Usually follows familiar conversational questions without repetition; unfamiliar abstractions may require clarification.
- **Grammar:** Uses present/past/future basics, comparatives and common modals; complex clause control is limited.
- **Expected answer shape:** simple question — 2–3 sentences; familiar open question — 4–7 linked sentences; stretch question — simplified 2–4 sentence response; may request clarification.
- **Error behaviour:** Noticeable grammar errors remain, especially under pressure, but seldom cause misunderstanding.
- **Upper boundary:** Limited subordinate-clause control and little nuance; difficult ideas are simplified rather than developed.

### Useful tester probes

- **narrative detail:** “Tell me about a time something went wrong on a trip or at school.”  
  Expected: sequence + basic cause/result.  
  Ceiling signal: little background, evaluation or complex time relation.
- **comparison:** “Compare learning online and learning in class.”  
  Expected: two-sided concrete comparison.  
  Ceiling signal: limited qualification or abstraction.
- **hypothesis:** “If you could change one school rule, what would you change?”  
  Expected: understands idea with a simple conditional-like response.  
  Ceiling signal: form/argument remains basic.

### Adjacent-band discrimination

- **Why not lower?** A2.1 is more fragmented and less reliable in short narratives.
- **Why not higher?** B1.1 can sustain connected speech, narrate more independently and explain a viewpoint beyond a few simple reasons.

## B1.1 — Early B1: maintains familiar conversation, narrates events and gives connected reasons, but complexity noticeably reduces control.

**Official anchor:** B1 (lower/emerging half)

### Five spoken-performance dimensions

- **Range:** Enough language to get by on familiar topics, with hesitation and circumlocution outside them.
- **Accuracy:** Frequently used routines/patterns are reasonably accurate; ambitious language destabilises control.
- **Fluency:** Comprehensible extended speech, but grammatical/lexical planning and repair are clearly visible.
- **Interaction:** Can initiate, maintain and close familiar conversation and confirm understanding.
- **Coherence:** Links shorter simple elements into a connected linear sequence.

### Typical interview behaviour

- **Comprehension:** Understands clear standard questions and follow-ups; dense or abstract questions may need reformulation.
- **Grammar:** Reasonable control of common tense contrasts, modals and first conditional; more complex forms are emerging.
- **Expected answer shape:** simple question — 2–4 sentences; familiar open question — 5–9 connected sentences; stretch question — 3–6 sentences with simplification, pauses and errors.
- **Error behaviour:** Persistent but non-systematic tense, agreement, article and preposition errors; complexity lowers accuracy.
- **Upper boundary:** Arguments lack detail/qualification; sustained hypotheticals and precise comparisons are inconsistent.

### Useful tester probes

- **narrative control:** “Tell me about a memorable day and why it was memorable.”  
  Expected: connected chronology + reaction.  
  Ceiling signal: background/detail/time relations remain simple.
- **hypothesis:** “What would happen if students had no homework?”  
  Expected: basic hypothetical idea, perhaps mixed forms.  
  Ceiling signal: difficulty sustaining consequences or alternatives.
- **opinion development:** “Is social media more helpful or harmful for teenagers?”  
  Expected: view + reasons/examples.  
  Ceiling signal: limited qualification/counterargument.

### Adjacent-band discrimination

- **Why not lower?** A2.2 produces shorter, more concrete accounts and has less independent control of narrative/opinion.
- **Why not higher?** B1.2 develops answers more flexibly, handles some hypothetical/inferential questions and keeps basic grammar more stable while taking risks.

## B1.2 — Strong B1: fairly confident connected speech with reasons, organised narratives and emerging complex language.

**Official anchor:** B1 (upper/consolidating half)

### Five spoken-performance dimensions

- **Range:** A solid familiar-topic repertoire with effective circumlocution and emerging complex sentence forms.
- **Accuracy:** Routine language is usually stable; errors are more associated with risk-taking and complexity.
- **Fluency:** Can keep a reasonably even flow on familiar topics, though planning remains evident in demanding stretches.
- **Interaction:** Maintains exchange confidently and can respond to less predictable follow-ups.
- **Coherence:** Produces connected linear discourse with clearer organisation and several common cohesive devices.

### Typical interview behaviour

- **Comprehension:** Follows most standard questioning, including some hypothetical and inferential prompts.
- **Grammar:** Common tense/aspect patterns are mostly controlled; relative clauses, conditionals and reported ideas appear with mixed accuracy.
- **Expected answer shape:** simple question — 3–5 sentences; familiar open question — 6–10 developed sentences; stretch question — 4–7 sentences with some complexity but visible lexical/grammatical strain.
- **Error behaviour:** Errors cluster in more ambitious structures; basic grammar is usually stable.
- **Upper boundary:** Precision, collocation, nuance and sustained abstract argument are not yet reliably B2.

### Useful tester probes

- **extended opinion:** “Should schools limit phone use? Give both sides and your view.”  
  Expected: multiple reasons/examples and some contrast.  
  Ceiling signal: counterargument/qualification remains simple or repetitive.
- **hypothetical chain:** “If you were headteacher for a week, what would you change and what might happen?”  
  Expected: sustains a basic hypothetical sequence.  
  Ceiling signal: tense/conditional control weakens as chain develops.
- **inference:** “Why might someone disagree with you?”  
  Expected: can imagine another perspective.  
  Ceiling signal: less precision/nuance than B2.

### Adjacent-band discrimination

- **Why not lower?** B1.1 is more hesitant and linear with less stable complex language.
- **Why not higher?** B2.1 shows more spontaneous detailed viewpoints, wider range and a fairly even tempo with fewer conspicuous planning pauses.

## B2.1 — Early B2: spontaneous interaction and detailed viewpoints with clear emerging sophistication, but precision and control are not fully consistent.

**Official anchor:** B2 (lower/emerging half)

### Five spoken-performance dimensions

- **Range:** Sufficient range for clear descriptions and viewpoints on most general topics, using some complex sentence forms.
- **Accuracy:** Relatively high grammatical control; mistakes rarely cause misunderstanding and many can be corrected.
- **Fluency:** Fairly even tempo with few noticeably long pauses, though searching is still visible.
- **Interaction:** Initiates discourse, takes turns appropriately and helps familiar discussion along.
- **Coherence:** Uses a limited but effective set of cohesive devices; longer contributions can still be slightly jumpy.

### Typical interview behaviour

- **Comprehension:** Understands extended natural questions and most implied meaning when the topic is accessible.
- **Grammar:** Good range of subordinate clauses, conditionals, passives and tense/aspect; errors remain noticeable in demanding stretches.
- **Expected answer shape:** simple question — 3–5 concise sentences; familiar open question — 7–12 developed sentences; stretch question — 5–9 sentences with argument, qualification and some searching.
- **Error behaviour:** Generally good control, with slips and recurring weaknesses in complex structures or collocations.
- **Upper boundary:** May become repetitive or imprecise when required to nuance, qualify or defend an abstract position at length.

### Useful tester probes

- **two-sided argument:** “What are the advantages and disadvantages of AI in education?”  
  Expected: clear structure, examples and position.  
  Ceiling signal: qualification/collocation may be repetitive or imprecise.
- **counterfactual:** “How would your life be different if you had grown up in another country?”  
  Expected: sustains hypothetical comparison.  
  Ceiling signal: fine tense/aspect control may wobble.
- **challenge:** “I disagree. Isn’t that argument too simple?”  
  Expected: defends/reframes position spontaneously.  
  Ceiling signal: less nuanced stance management than strong B2/C1.

### Adjacent-band discrimination

- **Why not lower?** B1.2 has less even fluency and a narrower complex-language repertoire.
- **Why not higher?** B2.2 is more consistently flexible, cohesive and controlled across abstract/hypothetical discussion.

## B2.2 — Strong B2: sustained, flexible conversation with detailed argument and relatively high control across familiar and abstract topics.

**Official anchor:** B2 (upper/consolidating half)

### Five spoken-performance dimensions

- **Range:** Broad general-topic range with complex sentence forms and effective paraphrase.
- **Accuracy:** High control overall; errors are mostly slips or local weaknesses and do not impede meaning.
- **Fluency:** Sustained fairly even flow with hesitation chiefly for formulation/ideas.
- **Interaction:** Flexible, spontaneous participation with initiative, repair and effective response to challenge.
- **Coherence:** Clear extended discourse using a useful variety of cohesive devices and organisational patterns.

### Typical interview behaviour

- **Comprehension:** Comfortable with most natural questioning, including abstract, hypothetical and evaluative prompts.
- **Grammar:** Wide structural range used spontaneously with good control; complex errors are occasional rather than dominant.
- **Expected answer shape:** simple question — 3–5 natural sentences; familiar open question — 8–14 developed sentences if invited; stretch question — 6–10 sentences with sustained argument and occasional precision limits.
- **Error behaviour:** Some slips, collocation issues and awkward formulations remain but communication is consistently effective.
- **Upper boundary:** Does not yet show consistently precise, nuanced and almost effortless control across unfamiliar domains expected at C1.

### Useful tester probes

- **nuance:** “Can something be both fair and unfair depending on perspective?”  
  Expected: handles qualification and examples.  
  Ceiling signal: lexical/idiomatic precision not consistently C1-like.
- **synthesis:** “Take two ideas we discussed and explain how they are connected.”  
  Expected: organises a clear synthesis.  
  Ceiling signal: less rhetorical flexibility than C1.
- **register/stance:** “How would you explain your view differently to a friend and to a headteacher?”  
  Expected: some awareness of register.  
  Ceiling signal: adaptation may be more conceptual than linguistically sophisticated.

### Adjacent-band discrimination

- **Why not lower?** B2.1 is a little less consistent in cohesion, control and precision under pressure.
- **Why not higher?** C1.1 maintains a high degree of accuracy and flexible expression with much less need to restrict or simplify what they want to say.

## C1.1 — Early C1: fluent and flexible in extended abstract discussion, with occasional limits in precision, register or idiomatic naturalness.

**Official anchor:** C1 (lower/emerging half)

### Five spoken-performance dimensions

- **Range:** Broad command allowing clear expression on wide general/academic/leisure topics without much restriction.
- **Accuracy:** Consistently high grammatical accuracy; errors are rare and often corrected.
- **Fluency:** Fluent and spontaneous, almost effortless; conceptual difficulty is the main source of disruption.
- **Interaction:** Uses discourse functions strategically to take/keep the floor and relate contributions skilfully.
- **Coherence:** Clear, well-structured extended speech with controlled organisation and cohesive devices.

### Typical interview behaviour

- **Comprehension:** Understands rapid, indirect and conceptually demanding questioning with little support.
- **Grammar:** Wide and varied complex grammar with high accuracy; errors are infrequent and often self-corrected.
- **Expected answer shape:** simple question — natural concise answer; familiar open question — extended response shaped to purpose; stretch question — sustained nuanced answer with occasional lexical/idiomatic searching.
- **Error behaviour:** Occasional subtle collocation, register or complex-structure errors; rarely distracting.
- **Upper boundary:** Nuance and idiomatic control are not yet consistently effortless across unfamiliar domains.

### Useful tester probes

- **qualification:** “When might a generally good rule produce a bad result?”  
  Expected: nuanced conditions/exceptions.  
  Ceiling signal: some lexical/idiomatic precision gaps may appear.
- **implicit meaning:** “What assumption is hidden in that question?”  
  Expected: identifies and discusses premise.  
  Ceiling signal: less effortless subtlety than upper C1/C2.
- **reframing:** “Can you make the opposite case convincingly?”  
  Expected: switches stance and reorganises argument.  
  Ceiling signal: occasional awkwardness in register or collocation.

### Adjacent-band discrimination

- **Why not lower?** B2.2 is highly effective but shows more restriction/repetition and less consistently high grammatical control.
- **Why not higher?** C1.2 is more consistently precise, natural and rhetorically controlled across unfamiliar or demanding topics.

## C1.2 — Strong C1: highly fluent, precise and well organised across demanding topics with strong register and discourse control.

**Official anchor:** C1 (upper/consolidating half)

### Five spoken-performance dimensions

- **Range:** Very broad flexible repertoire with precise reformulation and strong register awareness.
- **Accuracy:** High grammatical control is sustained even in complex extended turns.
- **Fluency:** Natural, smooth flow with hesitation mainly for genuinely difficult ideas.
- **Interaction:** Strategically manages floor, stance, alignment and clarification with ease.
- **Coherence:** Smooth, well-structured discourse using varied organisational and cohesive resources.

### Typical interview behaviour

- **Comprehension:** Handles implicit meaning, humour, nuance and complex questions reliably.
- **Grammar:** Very wide range with consistently high control and natural variation.
- **Expected answer shape:** simple question — economical and natural; familiar open question — extended only as needed; stretch question — sustained nuanced response with rare minor searching.
- **Error behaviour:** Rare slips that are minor and usually repaired.
- **Upper boundary:** May still lack the near-complete stylistic/idiomatic command and extremely fine semantic precision associated with C2.

### Useful tester probes

- **fine distinction:** “What is the difference between being confident and being certain?”  
  Expected: precise semantic contrast with examples.  
  Ceiling signal: rare lexical nuance gap may appear.
- **rhetorical control:** “Give the same argument first neutrally, then persuasively.”  
  Expected: noticeable register/rhetorical shift.  
  Ceiling signal: may be slightly less idiomatically effortless than C2.
- **ambiguity:** “Give two interpretations of an ambiguous statement and defend each.”  
  Expected: handles ambiguity flexibly.  
  Ceiling signal: C2-level compression/allusion not consistently present.

### Adjacent-band discrimination

- **Why not lower?** C1.1 can show occasional precision/register limitations under demanding conditions.
- **Why not higher?** C2.1 shows near-mastery: finer shades of meaning, idiomatic flexibility and almost invisible repair.

## C2.1 — Near-mastery C2: exceptionally flexible, precise and nuanced communication with very few detectable limitations.

**Official anchor:** C2 (lower/emerging half)

### Five spoken-performance dimensions

- **Range:** Very great flexibility in reformulating ideas to convey fine shades of meaning with strong idiomatic command.
- **Accuracy:** Consistent control of complex language even while planning and monitoring interaction.
- **Fluency:** Spontaneous extended speech with difficulty avoided or repaired so smoothly it is barely noticeable.
- **Interaction:** Ease and skill in natural turn-taking, reference, allusion and stance management.
- **Coherence:** Highly coherent/cohesive discourse using a wide range of organisational patterns and connectors.

### Typical interview behaviour

- **Comprehension:** Understands essentially all interview input, including compressed, figurative or culturally loaded language when context permits.
- **Grammar:** Near-complete control of complex grammar, including marked and stylistically motivated structures.
- **Expected answer shape:** simple question — precise and economical; familiar open question — fully natural amount of detail; stretch question — sophisticated sustained response with virtually invisible repair.
- **Error behaviour:** Very rare slips, usually comparable to proficient speakers generally.
- **Upper boundary:** May occasionally choose a slightly less natural idiom/register option than the simulator’s top C2.2 band.

### Useful tester probes

- **semantic precision:** “Distinguish two near-synonymous ideas in a context of your choice.”  
  Expected: fine-grained distinction and reformulation.  
  Ceiling signal: only very subtle naturalness difference from top band.
- **allusion/implication:** “Respond to an indirect or deliberately ambiguous challenge.”  
  Expected: tracks implication and stance effortlessly.  
  Ceiling signal: rarely may choose less elegant wording.
- **style shifting:** “Recast a complex idea for a child, a friend and an academic audience.”  
  Expected: strong controlled style shifts.  
  Ceiling signal: near-mastery throughout.

### Adjacent-band discrimination

- **Why not lower?** C1.2 is highly advanced but less consistently idiomatic/fine-grained.
- **Why not higher?** C2.2 is the simulator’s maximal band: practically no discernible language limitation in interview conditions.

## C2.2 — Top simulator band: consistently precise, natural, nuanced and adaptable spoken English within ordinary interview conditions.

**Official anchor:** C2 (upper/consolidating half)

### Five spoken-performance dimensions

- **Range:** Exceptionally broad and flexible, including fine shades of meaning, idiom, colloquialism and register shifts.
- **Accuracy:** Consistent grammatical control regardless of complexity or competing attentional demands.
- **Fluency:** Natural colloquial flow at length with seamless reformulation.
- **Interaction:** Effortless, subtle turn-taking and stance management with full sensitivity to implication.
- **Coherence:** Fully controlled organisation and cohesion using an extensive repertoire of discourse resources.

### Typical interview behaviour

- **Comprehension:** Virtually unrestricted in ordinary interview conditions, including implication, humour and rapid reformulation.
- **Grammar:** Consistently effortless structural control with stylistic flexibility.
- **Expected answer shape:** simple question — precise, natural and proportionate; familiar open question — fully natural response length; stretch question — sophisticated answer without artificial display of complexity.
- **Error behaviour:** Only incidental slips; do not manufacture conspicuous learner errors.
- **Upper boundary:** Top target band in this simulator.

### Useful tester probes

- **fine shades:** “Explain a subtle distinction, then reformulate it twice without losing meaning.”  
  Expected: precise flexible reformulation.  
  Ceiling signal: no intended ceiling inside simulator.
- **rhetorical agility:** “Argue a position, concede the strongest objection, then rebuild the case.”  
  Expected: fully controlled stance and discourse.  
  Ceiling signal: no intended ceiling inside simulator.
- **implicit interaction:** “Use an indirect challenge or ironic premise and see whether the learner tracks it.”  
  Expected: recognises and handles implication naturally.  
  Ceiling signal: no intended ceiling inside simulator.

### Adjacent-band discrimination

- **Why not lower?** C2.1 may show an exceptionally subtle occasional naturalness or idiomatic limitation.
- **Why not higher?** No higher simulator band.
